About

Post-production coordination across production companies, edit suites and platforms: master scheduling, asset management, technical conform and quality control under the standards of global streamers. Alongside that, writing original formats and curating content.

A degree in Film (UFF) and a postgraduate specialization in Screenwriting (AIC), combined with a technical, aesthetic and strategic profile. In the workflow, AI tools come in as accelerators for post: automated transcription and logging, text-based editing and assisted quality control, always in service of the technical standard and of the story.

Writing

  • 01 Ex-TV Nerd Medium · TV criticism · in Portuguese (opens in a new tab)

    A read on the 2010s split by window (broadcast, basic cable, premium cable and streaming), because each one produces under a different logic, and measuring them all with the same ruler is the habit awards and criticism keep repeating. The thesis: elevating TV to art froze a single standard of quality, the expensive production with cinematic language and a marquee name on the credits. Between arguments, the market math: billion-dollar showrunner deals, the $300 million behind The Morning Show and the debt Netflix ran up chasing catalogue.

  • 02 Quality TV Thesis · HBO / Enlightened · in Portuguese (opens in a new tab)

    A thesis that turns the notion of quality television inside out. In criticism and in the market the label almost always sticks to drama; here the object is comedy on American pay TV: the history, the concept and the social function of the genre, with HBO at the center for being the channel that consolidated the term in its own institutional discourse. The title carries the provocation: when did comedies become 30-minute dramas?

  • 03 The HBO Rupture Paper · discourse of differentiation · in Portuguese (opens in a new tab)

    Enlightened (HBO, 2011) as a limit case: a dramedy that exposes the market paradoxes of contemporary pay TV and the cost of sustaining a discourse of differentiation. The article unpacks how the channel built a brand on “it’s not TV” and what happens when its own product does not fit the ruler it created.
